1. PROJECT OVERVIEW & OBJECTIVES
================================================================================

The Fuel Fighter Driving Strategy project optimizes the driving behavior of a 
lightweight race car to minimize total energy consumption over a race 
circuit while respecting physical and mechanical constraints.

KEY OBJECTIVES:
- Minimize propulsion energy consumption per lap
- Maximize average speed subject to energy constraints
- Generate safe, realizable driving trajectories
- Account for 3D track geometry (elevation changes)
- Respect vehicle powertrain limits (gearbox, motor, friction)
- Maintain safety margins on tire grip

The system uses two complementary optimization approaches:
  1. Optimal Control Problem (OCP): Finds energy-optimal path and velocity
  2. Pulse-and-Glide Strategy: Energy-efficient speed modulation heuristic

================================================================================
2. SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE & WORKFLOW
================================================================================

The system follows this computational pipeline:

MAIN FLOW (driving_strat/main.py):
  
  Phase 1: Track Data Loading
  ├─ Load CSV containing distance, coordinates (UTM), and elevation
  ├─ Compute track geometry: center line, left/right boundaries
  ├─ Shift coordinates to origin (0, 0)
  └─ Build continuous splines for x, y, heading (psi), curvature (kappa)

  Phase 2: 3D Optimization Problem Setup
  ├─ Define OCP in CasADi (symbolic optimization)
  ├─ Variables: lateral deviation (n), heading (alpha), velocity (v),
  │            acceleration (accel), wheel force (F_wheels)
  ├─ Objective: Minimize energy + penalties for steering/path roughness
  └─ Constraints: Vehicle dynamics, friction ellipse, motor limits, bounds

  Phase 3: Alternating Optimization (2 iterations)
  ├─ Iteration 1: Solve OCP to find energy-optimal path and speed
  ├─ Extract optimized curvature from lateral path
  ├─ Smooth curvature to prevent unrealistic steering demands
  ├─ Iteration 2: Use smoothed curvature for next OCP solve
  └─ Target velocity updated based on achieved average speed

  Phase 4: Pulse-and-Glide Strategy
  ├─ Refine velocity profile using heuristic pulse-and-glide control
  ├─ Compute speed caps from cornering grip
  ├─ Simulate forward acceleration (pulse) phase
  ├─ Compute backward glide envelope (passive deceleration)
  ├─ Merge to find feasible speed envelope
  └─ Select gears and check powertrain feasibility

  Phase 5: Results Analysis & Visualization
  ├─ Transform lateral coordinates → Cartesian path
  ├─ Compute acceleration and energy profiles
  ├─ Print performance metrics (energy, lap time, gear shifts, etc.)
  └─ Generate 4-panel visualization:
      - Track with color-coded driving mode (pulse vs glide)
      - Acceleration profile
      - Force/drive profile
      - Elevation profile

CORE MODULES:

  model/car_model.py ............... Physical vehicle model
  track/track_loader.py ............ CSV data loading and geometry
  track/curvature.py ............... B-spline track interpolation
  optimization/ocp.py .............. CasADi optimal control formulation
  optimization/pulse_glide.py ....... Heuristic pulse-and-glide strategy
  optimization/warmstart.py ......... Initial guess generation for OCP
  utils/plotting.py ................. Visualization utilities

3. VEHICLE MODEL & PHYSICS
================================================================================

The vehicle model (model/car_model.py) defines a lightweight race car with:

MASS & AERODYNAMICS:
  Mass (m) ......................... 150 kg (80 kg car + 70 kg driver)
  Frontal Area (A) ................. 0.7651 m²
  Drag Coefficient (Cd) ............ 0.15
  Aerodynamic Drag Area (CdA) ...... 0.115 m²
  Air Density (ρ) .................. 1.225 kg/m³
  Rolling Resistance Coefficient ... 0.005

KEY FORCES (at velocity v):

  Aerodynamic Drag:
    F_drag = 0.5 * ρ * CdA * v²

  Rolling Resistance (on slope θ):
    F_rr = m * g * cos(θ) * Crr

  Gravity Component (on slope θ):
    F_gravity = m * g * sin(θ)
    
  Lateral Tire Force (in corner):
    F_lat = m * v² * κ
    where κ = track curvature

POWERTRAIN:
  Motor Max Torque ................. 6.8 Nm
  Motor Max Power .................. 2600 W
  Motor Max RPM .................... 4200 RPM
  
  Gear Ratios (4-speed gearbox):
    G1: 3.6    (acceleration-focused)
    G2: 2.4
    G3: 1.6
    G4: 1.15   (top-speed-focused)
  
  Final Drive Ratio ................ 2.8
  Drivetrain Efficiency ............ 95%
  Wheel Radius ..................... 0.26 m

  Gear Shift Behavior:
    Upshift Trigger ................ 3600 RPM
    Downshift Trigger .............. 2200 RPM
    Shift Duration ................. 0.20 s
    Torque Cut During Shift ........ 50% reduction

POWER & FORCE LIMITS:
  Torque at wheels = Motor_torque * Gear_ratio * Final_drive / Wheel_radius
  Power at wheels = Motor_power
  Max Longitudinal Force = min(Torque_limit, Power_limit)

FRICTION ELLIPSE:
  Total available tire grip is limited by friction coefficient (μ).
  Lateral force (cornering) and longitudinal force (acceleration/braking)
  compete for the same tire contact:
  
    (F_long / F_max)² + (F_lat / (μ * N))² ≤ 1.0
    
  where N = m * g * cos(θ) is the normal force on a slope θ.

4. TRACK GEOMETRY & CURVATURE
================================================================================

TRACK LOADING (track/track_loader.py):
  - Reads CSV with columns: Distance, UTMX, UTMY, Elevation (m)
  - Coordinates shifted so first point = (0, 0)
  - Track boundaries computed 6 m left/right of center line
  
  Output: s, x, y, xl, yl, xr, yr, z
    s: Distance along track [m]
    x, y: Center line coordinates [m]
    xl, yl: Left boundary [m]
    xr, yr: Right boundary [m]
    z: Elevation [m]

CURVATURE COMPUTATION (track/curvature.py):
  - Builds cubic B-splines for x(s), y(s), ψ(s)
  - Heading ψ(s) computed from dx/dy and unwrapped to ensure continuity
  - Curvature κ(s) = dψ/ds (derivative of heading)
  - Splines are C² continuous (smooth curvature for solver)
  
  Discrete curvature formula:
    κ = (dx/ds * d²y/ds² - dy/ds * d²x/ds²) / (dx/ds² + dy/ds²)^1.5

5. OPTIMAL CONTROL PROBLEM (OCP) FORMULATION
================================================================================

The OCP solves for energy-optimal path and velocity using CasADi + IPOPT.

VARIABLES (discretized over 300 points):
  n[i] ............. Lateral deviation from center [m, bounded ±6 m]
  α[i] ............. Heading error [rad, bounded ±15°]
  v[i] ............. Velocity [m/s, bounded 0-15 m/s]
  accel[i] ......... Acceleration [m/s², bounded -8 to +3.5 m/s²]
  F_wheels[i] ...... Longitudinal drive force [N, bounded 0-250 N]

OBJECTIVE FUNCTION (Minimize):
  E_prop + E_grav + λ₁*E_speed + λ₂*E_steer + λ₃*E_path + λ₄*E_force + λ₅*E_jerk
  
  where:
    E_prop = Σ max(0, F_wheels) * ds  [Propulsion energy]
    E_grav = m*g*Σ max(0, Δz)         [Gravity (uphill cost)]
    E_speed = 0.5*Σ(v - v_target)²*ds [Speed tracking penalty]
    E_steer = 5.0*Σ(dα/ds)²*ds        [Smooth steering]
    E_path = 10.0*Σ(d²n/ds²)²*ds      [Smooth lateral path]
    E_force = 0.1*Σ(ΔF)²              [Smooth force application]
    E_jerk = 2.0*Σ(Δaccel)²           [Smooth acceleration]

CONSTRAINTS:

  Dynamics:
    v[i+1]² = v[i]² + 2*accel[i]*ds
    accel[i] = (F - F_drag - F_rr - F_gravity) / m
    
  Friction Ellipse:
    (F_wheels / F_max)² + (F_lat / (μ * N))² ≤ 1.0
    where F_lat = m * v² * κ
    
  Motor/Powertrain:
    F_wheels[i] ≤ max_drive_force(v[i])
    
  Bounds:
    -6 ≤ n ≤ 6
    -15° ≤ α ≤ 15°
    0 ≤ v ≤ 15
    -8 ≤ accel ≤ 3.5
    
  Boundary Conditions:
    v[0] = 0, v[N] = 0 (start/finish at rest)
    n[0] = n[N], α[0] = α[N] (closed loop)

SOLVER SETTINGS (IPOPT):
  Max iterations: 500
  Tolerance: 1e-2
  Hessian: Limited-memory
  Acceptable tolerance: 1e-1 (allows early convergence)

6. PULSE-AND-GLIDE STRATEGY
================================================================================

After OCP, a heuristic pulse-and-glide controller refines the velocity profile.

CONCEPT:
  "Pulse": Apply constant acceleration (e.g., 1.3 m/s²) until reaching a 
           speed ceiling determined by tire grip in corners.
  "Glide": Release throttle (F = 0), coast, and decelerate via drag + rolling.

ALGORITHM:

  1. Speed Cap Calculation:
     For each point, max speed limited by cornering grip:
       v_cap[i] = sqrt(μ * g / κ[i])
     Apply backward smoothing to prevent sharp deceleration demands.

  2. Forward Simulation (Pulse Phase):
     For each segment, determine if pulsing or gliding:
       - Below v_low = 0.82 * v_cap: PULSE (accelerate)
       - Above v_high = 0.96 * v_cap: GLIDE (coast)
     Apply hysteresis to prevent rapid switching.

  3. Force Calculation:
     Friction ellipse limits longitudinal force:
       F_lat = m * v² * κ
       F_max_tire = sqrt((μ*N)² - F_lat²)
     Powertrain limit:
       F_max_powertrain = gear_force_at_current_speed
     Control force:
       F_wheels = min(F_max_tire, F_max_powertrain)
     During pulse: F = min(m*a_pulse + F_resist, F_max)
     During glide: F = 0

  4. Backward Glide Envelope:
     Maximum speed that can decelerate to endpoint on passive drag alone:
       v_glide[k] = sqrt(v_glide[k+1]² + 2*a_decel*ds)
     where a_decel is from drag + rolling resistance (no motor braking).

  5. Final Envelope:
     v_final = min(v_forward, v_glide, v_local_cap)

  6. Gear Selection:
     For each segment, select gear with highest available force.
     Track gear shifts (upshift/downshift RPM thresholds).

KEY PARAMETERS:
  mu_tire ...................... 0.75 (tire friction)
  pulse_accel .................. 1.3 m/s² (target acceleration in pulse phase)
  low_ratio .................... 0.82 (lower threshold: 82% of speed cap)
  high_ratio ................... 0.96 (upper threshold: 96% of speed cap)

ADVANTAGES:
  - Computationally fast (no solver needed)
  - Physically intuitive (realistic driving pattern)
  - Respects all constraints: motor, gearing, grip, slope
  - Includes gear shift dynamics and efficiency losses

9. PHYSICAL INSIGHTS & ENERGY OPTIMIZATION STRATEGY
================================================================================

CORE ENERGY OPTIMIZATION PRINCIPLES:

  1. Minimize Propulsion Work:
     Energy = Integral of Power = Integral of (Force × velocity)
     Only the motor adds energy; gravity and drag remove it.
     Strategy: Pulse when speed is low (work per unit distance is less),
              glide when speed is high (let drag/slope do the work).

  2. Leverage Gravity on Downhill:
     F_gravity = m*g*sin(θ) (positive downhill, negative uphill)
     The solver naturally accelerates downhill without throttle.
     Uphill slopes increase required power, so early acceleration is strategic.

  3. Cornering Grip Trade-off:
     In tight corners, curvature increases F_lat = m*v²*κ
     Available longitudinal force decreases: F_long = sqrt((μ*N)² - F_lat²)
     Strategy: Slow down in corners to free up grip for acceleration.
              Accelerate on straights where curvature is low.

  4. Speed Regulation:
     Optimal speed is a balance:
     - Higher speed → Higher drag (quadratic) → More propulsion needed
     - Lower speed → Longer lap time → More energy on overall journey
     The solver finds the sweet spot via the speed-tracking penalty.

  5. Acceleration Efficiency:
     Efficiency = Propulsion work / Total energy
     Brief, smooth accelerations (1-3 m/s²) are more efficient than 
     aggressive jerky ones (5+ m/s²).
     Path smoothness penalties (steering + path penalties) enforce this.

  6. Powertrain Efficiency:
     Motor power = Torque × ω_rpm
     Best efficiency occurs at moderate RPM (2000-3500) in mid-range gears.
     The model includes gear-ratio selection to maintain optimal operating point.

EXAMPLE DRIVING PATTERN (typical lap):

  Straight 1-2 (low curvature, downhill):
    → PULSE: Accelerate hard (1.5+ m/s²)
    → Speed builds to 10-12 m/s as road descends
    
  Turn 3 (tight corner, κ high, flat):
    → Brake to 6-8 m/s (grip limit)
    → No propulsion (F=0) or minimal
    
  Straight 4 (mild uphill):
    → PULSE: Moderate acceleration (0.8 m/s²)
    → Fight gravity: F = m*a + m*g*sin(θ) + F_drag
    
  Downhill 5 (steep decline, well below apex):
    → GLIDE: Accelerate for free via gravity
    → Velocity limited by drag and upcoming corner
    
  Exit 6 (medium curve, slight downhill):
    → Build speed from gravity while managing cornering limits
    → Transition to pulse if additional acceleration is beneficial

10. TROUBLESHOOTING & NOTES
================================================================================

OCP Convergence Issues:
  If "OCP debug trajectory" appears instead of "OCP converged":
  - Reduce steering weight or path smoothness penalties
  - Increase IPOPT tolerance
  - Provide better initial guess (warmstart.py adjustment)
  - Check track data for discontinuities or NaN values

Unrealistic Spiraling/Oscillatory paths:
  - Increase steering_smoothness coefficient (5.0 → 10.0)
  - Increase path_smoothness coefficient (10.0 → 20.0)
  - Reduce speed_tracking penalty (0.5 → 0.1)

Motor/Powertrain Violations:
  Check feasible flag in output. If False:
  - Reduce target_v (lower speed = lower required power)
  - Increase mu_tire (more grip margin in corners)
  - Verify gear ratios and motor limits are realistic

Large Gear Shifts Near Start/End:
  - Modify warmstart initial speed guess
  - Adjust upshift/downshift RPM thresholds
  - Increase shift_time_s to reduce aggressive shifting

Lap Time Discrepancies:
  Lap time = Integral(ds / v)
  If unexpected:
  - Check that speed profile is physically reasonable
  - Verify track distance is correct (compare s[0] to s[-1])
  - Confirm boundary conditions (v[0]=0, v[N]=0)
